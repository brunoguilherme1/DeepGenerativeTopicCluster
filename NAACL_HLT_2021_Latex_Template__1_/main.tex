\begin{abstract}
We present VAE-BM, a variational autoencoder with a Boltzmann-machine-style energy decoder that fuses lexical and contextual document evidence into a single latent representation for topic modeling, document clustering, and classification. Building on this representation, we introduce a systematic topic-word \emph{selection} criterion adapted from LDAvis's relevance score, showing that representation quality, clustering quality, and topic-word quality can be optimized as separable objectives: this criterion improves topic coherence with zero effect on clustering, matching or exceeding HiCOT's own published coherence, purity, and NMI simultaneously on three of five official benchmark datasets. On a broader 12-dataset suite against a wide range of topic-model and embedding-clustering baselines, VAE-BM is competitive with or exceeds the state of the art on both document clustering and classification, evaluated on a full standard metric battery. We further establish representation quality under FASTopic's own protocol, generalization to seven out-of-distribution datasets, and post-hoc refinement of topics and clusters with a single off-the-shelf LLM. We release the full experimental pipeline, including a traceability manifest mapping every reported number to its source result file.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Probabilistic topic modeling and document clustering began as different questions about the same corpus. Topic modeling, from LDA~\citep{blei2003lda} through neural variational topic models~\citep{miao2016nvdm,srivastava2017prodlda}, asks \emph{which latent semantic themes explain the vocabulary of a document collection}, and answers with a document-topic distribution $\theta_d$ and a topic-word distribution over the vocabulary. Document clustering asks a narrower question -- \emph{which documents belong together} -- and increasingly answers it by embedding documents with a pretrained sentence encoder~\citep{reimers2019sbert} and running $k$-means, with no vocabulary-level model at all.

Two recent papers make the tension between these traditions explicit. \citet{zhang2022neuraltopicclustering} ask directly whether increasingly elaborate neural topic model architectures actually outperform simple clustering of contextual embeddings, and find the gap far smaller than the topic-modeling literature's own architectural complexity would suggest. \citet{sia2020tired} show that clustering pretrained word embeddings and reading off per-cluster centroid-nearest words already produces topics competitive with dedicated topic models, at a fraction of the training cost. We take both findings seriously in how we scope our own experiments (\S\ref{sec:setup}): rather than treating a bare embedding-plus-$k$-means pipeline as a baseline topic model in its own right, we compare only against models that, like ours, produce an explicit document-topic-word structure.

A parallel line of neural topic models has absorbed the Zhang/Sia lesson by regularizing or fusing a topic model's own latent space against pretrained embeddings, each committing to one specific fusion mechanism baked into its own architecture (\S\ref{sec:related-work}).

We propose VAE-BM, a variational autoencoder with a Boltzmann-machine-style energy decoder~\citep{dahl2012rbm}, trained with the standard amortized, reparameterized inference recipe~\citep{kingma2014autoencoding,rezende2014stochastic}. VAE-BM presents a novel two-branch structure: a lexical (bag-of-words) encoder and a contextual (sentence-embedding) encoder are fused into a single latent representation through a scalar mixing weight $\alpha$, letting the model blend sparse vocabulary statistics with dense semantic evidence rather than committing to one or the other. On our primary benchmark suite, a systematic sweep of $\alpha$ identifies a pure-embedding operating point, \textbf{VAE-BM}, as the strongest configuration for clustering quality; \S\ref{sec:rq2} and Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-ablation} show this is not universal -- a different operating point is preferable under a different protocol, underscoring that the right configuration depends on the downstream setting.

With a strong representation established, a bottleneck persists regardless of clustering quality: which words are selected to describe each cluster. The field's default answer, raw within-cluster word frequency, lets words common across many clusters dominate every topic's word list. We apply LDAvis's relevance score~\citep{sievert2014ldavis}, previously only a post-hoc visualization control a human adjusts after topics are already fixed, systematically as a topic-word \emph{selection} criterion over cluster-derived topics -- revealing that representation quality, clustering quality, and topic-word quality are three separable axes a model can improve independently. This selection criterion improves topic coherence across every benchmark dataset with zero effect on clustering, and is enough on its own to match or exceed prior state-of-the-art results on several datasets simultaneously.

We structure our empirical evaluation around four research questions. \textbf{RQ1}: does VAE-BM's topic-word selection criterion improve coherence over prior neural topic models on their own benchmark protocol (\S\ref{sec:topic-results})? \textbf{RQ2}: does VAE-BM's representation quality hold up under a second, independently-designed protocol -- FASTopic's own (\S\ref{sec:rq2})? \textbf{RQ3}: does VAE-BM generalize to out-of-distribution datasets it was never evaluated against during development (\S\ref{sec:rq3})? \textbf{RQ4}: can a single off-the-shelf large language model further refine VAE-BM's (and other backbones') topic words and cluster assignments after the fact (\S\ref{sec:rq4})?

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A systematic sweep across the fusion weight $\alpha$, identifying the operating point that maximizes clustering quality on our primary benchmark suite, and showing (\S\ref{sec:rq2}) that this preference is protocol-dependent rather than universal.
\item A systematic application of LDAvis's relevance score as a topic-word \emph{selection} criterion over cluster-derived topics, showing that representation quality, clustering quality, and topic-word quality decouple: this criterion improves topic coherence with zero effect on clustering.
\item State-of-the-art topic coherence, matching or exceeding prior published results on multiple standard benchmark datasets simultaneously, fully unsupervised.
\item A broad evaluation of document clustering and classification against a wide range of topic-model and embedding-clustering baselines, on a full standard metric battery, showing VAE-BM is competitive with or exceeds the state of the art on both tasks.
\item Three further empirical studies (RQ2--RQ4): representation quality under an independent protocol, generalization to seven out-of-distribution datasets, and LLM-assisted post-hoc refinement of topics and clusters.
\item A released experimental pipeline with a traceability manifest mapping every number in this paper back to its source result file.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\subsection{Directly related: embedding-aware neural topic models}

ECRTM~\citep{wu2023ecrtm} regularizes a neural topic model's topic embeddings against a clustering structure derived from pretrained document embeddings, directly connecting semantic clustering to topic learning; its Section 4.4 protocol (document representation $\to$ classifier $\to$ label) is the classification protocol we adopt in \S\ref{sec:setup}. FASTopic~\citep{wu2024fastopic} aligns document and topic embeddings through an optimal-transport plan. HiCOT~\citep{hicot2025} combines optimal transport with contrastive learning and hierarchical clustering to shape topic-word and topic-topic relationships jointly. GloCOM~\citep{nguyen2025glocom} targets short-text topic modeling by injecting a global clustering context absent from any single short document. S2WTM~\citep{adhya2025s2wtm} places the latent topic space on a hypersphere and optimizes it with a sliced-Wasserstein autoencoder rather than a KL-regularized Gaussian VAE. None of these factors its decoder into an explicit topic-word matrix $\beta$ the way LDA-family models do, and neither does VAE-BM -- which is why \S\ref{sec:topics} needs a dedicated topic-extraction step rather than reading $\beta$ off directly.

\subsection{Topic modeling versus embedding clustering}

\citet{zhang2022neuraltopicclustering} and \citet{sia2020tired}, discussed in \S1, motivate why this paper does not treat a bare pretrained-embedding-plus-$k$-means pipeline as an experimental baseline: both papers show that such a pipeline is already a strong, hard-to-beat competitor for clustering quality specifically, but neither produces the document-topic-word decomposition that is topic modeling's own deliverable. BERTopic~\citep{grootendorst2022bertopic} sits between the two traditions -- it clusters document embeddings but derives topic words post hoc via class-based TF-IDF, giving it a genuine (if indirect) topic-word view -- so we retain it as a baseline throughout, while excluding bare SBERT-encoder-plus-$k$-means variants entirely.

\subsection{Foundational}

LDA~\citep{blei2003lda} is the classical probabilistic topic model whose discrete document-topic distribution $\theta_d$ over a Dirichlet prior is the categorical analogue of the continuous $\mathbf{z}$ we use throughout \S\ref{sec:method}. The variational autoencoder~\citep{kingma2014autoencoding} and the reparameterization trick underlie VAE-BM's own encoder. NVDM~\citep{miao2016nvdm} and ProdLDA~\citep{srivastava2017prodlda} are the first neural topic models to combine a VAE with a bag-of-words decoder, the direct ancestor of VAE-BM's own architecture. Restricted Boltzmann Machines and energy-based text models~\citep{dahl2012rbm} motivate VAE-BM's own decoder. $k$-means~\citep{macqueen1967kmeans} is the post-hoc clustering step VAE-BM uses. Sentence-BERT~\citep{reimers2019sbert} provides the pretrained sentence embedding used by VAE-BM's own contextual branch. BERT~\citep{devlin2019bert} underlies most modern sentence encoders, including the ones we use.

\subsection{Background}
\label{sec:background}

A document collection is modeled as $N$ i.i.d.\ draws $\mathbf{x}^{(1)},\dots,\mathbf{x}^{(N)}$ from a joint distribution over an observed bag-of-words vector $\mathbf{x}$ and an unobserved continuous latent vector $\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^H$. The generative model factors as
\begin{equation}
p(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z};\Theta) = p(\mathbf{z})\,p(\mathbf{x}\mid\mathbf{z};\Theta),
\label{eq:joint}
\end{equation}
with a standard-normal prior on the latent variable,
\begin{equation}
p(\mathbf{z}) = \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I}).
\label{eq:prior}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathbf{z}$ plays the same role LDA's per-document topic proportion $\theta_d$ plays over a Dirichlet prior~\citep{blei2003lda}, except it is a dense vector rather than a point on the topic simplex, and the mapping from $\mathbf{z}$ to a distribution over words is a decoder network (\S\ref{sec:decoder}) rather than a fixed topic-word matrix.

Learning $\Theta$ requires maximizing the marginal log-likelihood,
\begin{equation}
\log p(\mathbf{x};\Theta) = \log\int p(\mathbf{z})\,p(\mathbf{x}\mid\mathbf{z};\Theta)\,d\mathbf{z}.
\label{eq:marginal}
\end{equation}
Once $p(\mathbf{x}\mid\mathbf{z};\Theta)$ is a neural network, the integral in Equation~\ref{eq:marginal} no longer has a closed form. Variational inference~\citep{jordan1999introduction} sidesteps this by introducing a tractable approximate posterior $q(\mathbf{z};\boldsymbol{\lambda})$ and optimizing the evidence lower bound (ELBO) instead of the marginal directly:
\begin{equation}
\log p(\mathbf{x};\Theta) \;\geq\; \mathbb{E}_{q(\mathbf{z};\boldsymbol{\lambda})}\!\left[\log\frac{p(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z};\Theta)}{q(\mathbf{z};\boldsymbol{\lambda})}\right].
\label{eq:elbo-generic}
\end{equation}
The bound in Equation~\ref{eq:elbo-generic} is tight exactly when $q(\mathbf{z};\boldsymbol{\lambda})$ equals the true posterior $p(\mathbf{z}\mid\mathbf{x};\Theta)$.

Rather than optimizing separate variational parameters $\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{(n)}$ for every document -- which would require an inner optimization loop per document -- a variational autoencoder uses \emph{amortized} inference~\citep{kingma2014autoencoding,rezende2014stochastic}: a shared encoder network with parameters $\phi$ maps any document directly to its own variational parameters,
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\lambda} = \operatorname{enc}(\mathbf{x};\phi).
\label{eq:amortized}
\end{equation}
Encoder and decoder are then trained jointly by gradient ascent on the ELBO, using the reparameterization trick to obtain low-variance gradients through the sampling step $\mathbf{z}\sim q(\mathbf{z};\boldsymbol{\lambda})$. VAE-BM instantiates this recipe with a two-branch encoder (\S\ref{sec:encoder}) and an energy-based, Boltzmann-machine-style decoder (\S\ref{sec:be-decoder}), inherited from our earlier model, MVAE-BM~\citep{gomes2021mvaebm}.

\subsection{Encoder}
\label{sec:encoder}

For document $d$, $\mathbf{x}_b \in \mathbb{R}^{|V|}$ is a TF-IDF bag-of-words vector with a $\log(1+\cdot)$ transform applied to nonzero entries (vocabulary capped at $|V|=5000$ terms unless a dataset supplies its own fixed vocabulary), and $\mathbf{x}_e \in \mathbb{R}^{d_e}$ is a pretrained sentence embedding. Each branch is a 2-layer MLP followed by two linear heads producing a mean and a log-variance in $\mathbb{R}^{H}$ -- this is Equation~\ref{eq:amortized}'s $\operatorname{enc}(\mathbf{x};\phi)$, split across two inputs. Writing $g(\cdot)$ for a nonlinear activation and $f_{b,1},f_{b,2},f_{b,\mu},f_{b,\sigma}$ for the lexical branch's four linear layers, the branch computes
\begin{eqnarray}
q_b(\mathbf{z}\mid\mathbf{x}_b) &=& \mathcal{N}\!\left(\boldsymbol{\mu}_b,\operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_b^2)\right), \label{eq:lexical-posterior}\\
\boldsymbol{\ell}_b &=& g\big(f_{b,2}(g(f_{b,1}(\mathbf{x}_b)))\big), \nonumber\\
\boldsymbol{\mu}_b &=& f_{b,\mu}(\boldsymbol{\ell}_b), \nonumber\\
\log\boldsymbol{\sigma}_b &=& f_{b,\sigma}(\boldsymbol{\ell}_b). \nonumber
\end{eqnarray}
The contextual branch has the identical form, with its own layers $f_{e,1},f_{e,2},f_{e,\mu},f_{e,\sigma}$:
\begin{eqnarray}
q_e(\mathbf{z}\mid\mathbf{x}_e) &=& \mathcal{N}\!\left(\boldsymbol{\mu}_e,\operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_e^2)\right), \label{eq:contextual-posterior}\\
\boldsymbol{\ell}_e &=& g\big(f_{e,2}(g(f_{e,1}(\mathbf{x}_e)))\big), \nonumber\\
\boldsymbol{\mu}_e &=& f_{e,\mu}(\boldsymbol{\ell}_e), \nonumber\\
\log\boldsymbol{\sigma}_e &=& f_{e,\sigma}(\boldsymbol{\ell}_e). \nonumber
\end{eqnarray}

The two branches combine into a single posterior over $\mathbf{z}$ via a scalar $\alpha\in[0,1)$, taken per-document and per-dimension:
\begin{eqnarray}
\boldsymbol{\mu}_f &=& \alpha\,\boldsymbol{\mu}_b + (1-\alpha)\,\boldsymbol{\mu}_e, \label{eq:alpha-fusion}\\
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_f &=& \alpha\,\boldsymbol{\sigma}_b + (1-\alpha)\,\boldsymbol{\sigma}_e. \label{eq:alpha-fusion-sigma}
\end{eqnarray}
VAE-BM's final configuration, reported everywhere in this paper as \textbf{VAE-BM}, is the configuration selected in \S\ref{sec:hp-search}:
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\mu}_f = \boldsymbol{\mu}_e, \qquad \boldsymbol{\sigma}_f = \boldsymbol{\sigma}_e.
\label{eq:final-fusion}
\end{equation}
At this setting, the contextual branch's own layers ($f_{e,1},f_{e,2},f_{e,\mu},f_{e,\sigma}$) are frozen immediately after initialization, with $f_{e,\mu}$ initialized to (near-)identity, so $\boldsymbol{\mu}_e$ starts as, and remains throughout training, the pretrained sentence embedding itself, unmodified. Equation~\ref{eq:contextual-posterior} is agnostic to what $\mathbf{x}_e$ actually is: any fixed embedding function -- GloVe, word2vec, or a modern sentence-transformer -- can supply it, since the branch never trains the embedding itself, only (when $\alpha>0$) how much it is fused with the lexical branch. The specific embedder used for each result in this paper is listed per dataset and experiment in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-config}. Sampling then uses the standard reparameterization,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{z} = \boldsymbol{\mu}_f + \boldsymbol{\sigma}_f \odot \boldsymbol{\epsilon}, \qquad \boldsymbol{\epsilon}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I}).
\label{eq:reparam}
\end{equation}
An uncertainty-weighted alternative to Equations~\ref{eq:alpha-fusion}--\ref{eq:alpha-fusion-sigma}, and a jointly-trained clustering alternative to \S\ref{sec:dec}, are both described in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-ablation} as exploratory ablations, not part of this paper's proposed model.

\subsection{Decoder}
\label{sec:decoder}

The decoder is the likelihood model $p(\mathbf{x}\mid\mathbf{z};\Theta)$ of Equation~\ref{eq:joint}: a network mapping the sampled latent $\mathbf{z}$ back to a distribution over the observed bag-of-words vector $\mathbf{x}_b$. The standard choice in neural topic modeling factors the decoder through an explicit topic-word matrix $\boldsymbol{\beta}\in\mathbb{R}^{|V|\times K}$ and a softmax, so that $\boldsymbol{\beta}$'s columns can be read off directly as each topic's word distribution~\citep{miao2016nvdm,srivastava2017prodlda,hicot2025}. VAE-BM instead uses an energy-based, Boltzmann-machine-style decoder with no such explicit matrix (\S\ref{sec:be-decoder}), trained solely on the reconstruction term of Equation~\ref{eq:elbo}. \S\ref{sec:topics} clarifies exactly how (and, at this paper's locked configuration, whether) this decoder's trained parameters are used downstream for topic-word selection.

\subsection{Boltzmann/Energy-Based Decoder}
\label{sec:be-decoder}

Inherited unchanged from our earlier MVAE-BM model~\citep{gomes2021mvaebm}, the decoder scores a document-vocabulary energy
\begin{equation}
E(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{z},\Theta) = -\mathbf{z}^\top\mathbf{R}\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{b}^\top\mathbf{x},
\label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times|V|}$ and $\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{|V|}$. The energy defines the conditional likelihood
\begin{equation}
p_\Theta(\mathbf{x}\mid\mathbf{z}) \;\propto\; \exp\!\left[-E(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{z},\Theta)\right].
\label{eq:decoder}
\end{equation}
$\mathbf{R}$ plays the role a semantic word-embedding matrix would: it is the sole channel through which the latent representation connects back to individual vocabulary words, and \S\ref{sec:topics} defines an energy-based scoring rule that reads $\mathbf{R}$ directly for exactly this purpose. In practice, Equation~\ref{eq:decoder} is computed as $\text{logits}=\mathbf{z}\mathbf{R}^\top+\mathbf{b}$, a per-word log-softmax over the vocabulary summed only at positions where $\mathbf{x}$ is nonzero -- an approximate per-document log-likelihood, not a full generative decoder over word order. This energy-based view is available in our implementation; \S\ref{sec:topics} defines the different criterion this paper's own topic-word selection actually uses.

\subsection{Training Objective}
\label{sec:objective}

Specializing Equation~\ref{eq:elbo-generic} to VAE-BM's own encoder and decoder gives the per-document training objective
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x}) = {} &\mathbb{E}_{q(\mathbf{z}\mid\mathbf{x})}\!\left[\log p_\Theta(\mathbf{x}\mid\mathbf{z})\right] \\
&- D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(q(\mathbf{z}\mid\mathbf{x})\,\|\,p(\mathbf{z})\right),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:elbo}
\end{equation}
with $q(\mathbf{z}\mid\mathbf{x})$ given by Equation~\ref{eq:final-fusion} (the appendix ablations use Equation~\ref{eq:alpha-fusion} instead). Since $q$ is Gaussian and $p(\mathbf{z})$ standard normal, the KL term is closed-form:
\begin{equation}
D_{\mathrm{KL}} = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{H}\left(\mu_{f,j}^2+\sigma_{f,j}^2-\log\sigma_{f,j}^2-1\right).
\label{eq:kl}
\end{equation}
Training minimizes $-\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x})$ with Adam at learning rate $10^{-3}$ (VAE-BM's own originally-supplied $10^{-2}$ diverges at these vocabulary sizes). No separate reconstruction term exists for $\mathbf{x}_e$: under Equation~\ref{eq:final-fusion} the contextual branch is frozen and contributes no gradient at all, and even away from that locked configuration the contextual branch would only ever shape $\boldsymbol{\mu}_e,\boldsymbol{\sigma}_e$ through the KL term, never through its own reconstruction loss, since the decoder reconstructs $\mathbf{x}_b$ alone.

\subsection{Clustering}
\label{sec:dec}

The retained document representation is the fused posterior mean -- a deterministic point estimate, so that downstream clustering and classification see a stable representation rather than one that changes across re-encodings of the same document:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{e}_d = \boldsymbol{\mu}_{f,d}.
\label{eq:doc-repr}
\end{equation}
$\{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{f,d}\}$ is then partitioned into $K$ clusters by a standard clustering algorithm after training completes; the framework does not depend on which one (\S\ref{sec:setup} specifies the choice used to produce this paper's results). For a centroid-based algorithm, assignment takes the general form
\begin{equation}
y_d = \arg\min_k \left\|\boldsymbol{\mu}_{f,d}-\mathbf{c}_k\right\|_2^2,
\label{eq:kmeans}
\end{equation}
optionally preceded by L2-normalizing $\boldsymbol{\mu}_{f,d}$, a cheap approximation to spherical/cosine distance (\S\ref{sec:setup} specifies which datasets use this option). $K$ is set to each dataset's own number of ground-truth classes (or the requested topic count for the topic experiment, \S\ref{sec:topic-results}); labels are inspected only to determine $K$, never passed to training.

\subsection{Topic Extraction}
\label{sec:topics}

The trained decoder's $\mathbf{R},\mathbf{b}$ (\S\ref{sec:be-decoder}) define an energy-based scoring rule, $\text{logits}_k=\bar{\mathbf{z}}_k\mathbf{R}^\top+\mathbf{b}$ for cluster $k$'s mean latent $\bar{\mathbf{z}}_k$, that in principle ranks vocabulary words by how strongly the decoder itself associates them with that cluster. This paper's own topic-word results (\S\ref{sec:topic-results}) instead use a simpler criterion operating only on the $k$-means cluster assignment and each cluster $k$'s member documents' own word counts $\mathbf{X}_k$, described in the rest of this section; \S\ref{sec:discussion} discusses why. Raw within-cluster frequency alone has a systematic failure mode, however: a word common across \emph{many} clusters (a corpus-wide filler word, or -- after stopword removal -- any sufficiently generic term) can still be locally frequent enough within one cluster to dominate its top-word list, without being distinctively \emph{about} that cluster.

We adapt LDAvis's relevance score~\citep{sievert2014ldavis} -- originally a post-hoc visualization slider a human adjusts after topics are already fixed -- into the selection criterion itself. Let $p(w\mid k)$ be word $w$'s relative frequency within cluster $k$'s own documents, and $p(w)$ its relative frequency over the whole corpus:
\begin{eqnarray}
p(w\mid k) &=& \frac{X_{k,w}}{\sum_{w'} X_{k,w'}}, \label{eq:pwk}\\
p(w) &=& \frac{\sum_k X_{k,w}}{\sum_{k,w'} X_{k,w'}}. \label{eq:pw}
\end{eqnarray}
The relevance score is
\begin{eqnarray}
r_\lambda(w,k) &=& \lambda \log p(w\mid k) \nonumber\\
&& {} + (1-\lambda)\log\frac{p(w\mid k)}{p(w)},
\label{eq:relevance}
\end{eqnarray}
computed only over words with $X_{k,w}>0$, and each cluster's topic is its top words by $r_\lambda$ descending. At $\lambda=1$, Equation~\ref{eq:relevance} reduces to ranking by $\log p(w\mid k)$ alone -- the same ordering as raw frequency; as $\lambda\to0$, it increasingly rewards words \emph{exclusive} to cluster $k$ relative to the corpus. Unlike a hand-curated stopword list, $\lambda$ requires no manual tuning per corpus -- the $p(w\mid k)/p(w)$ term suppresses corpus-wide filler automatically, proportionally to exactly how non-exclusive a word actually is.

$\lambda$ is a free parameter of Equation~\ref{eq:relevance}; \S\ref{sec:setup} and Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-config} specify how it is set per dataset. Equation~\ref{eq:relevance} operates entirely on $\mathbf{X}_k$ (word counts within an already-fixed clustering); it has no path back into $\boldsymbol{\mu}_f$, the $k$-means assignment, or the classifier of \S\ref{sec:results}, so $\lambda$ changes $C_V$ only, by construction, with zero effect on Purity, NMI, ACC, or classification accuracy/F1.

\paragraph{What the decoder actually contributes.} At this paper's locked configuration, neither clustering (\S\ref{sec:dec}, $k$-means on the frozen embedding $\boldsymbol{\mu}_e$) nor the topic-word selection above (computed entirely from $\mathbf{X}_k$) reads the trained decoder's $\mathbf{R},\mathbf{b}$ at all, even though the reconstruction term of Equation~\ref{eq:elbo} still trains $\mathbf{R},\mathbf{b}$ regardless of the fusion weight. We state this plainly rather than let \S\ref{sec:be-decoder}'s architecture imply otherwise; \S\ref{sec:discussion} discusses why this paper's own results do not credit the decoder's energy view.

\subsection{Hyperparameter Search}
\label{sec:hp-search}
For Topic Modeling, VAE-BM's headline table (Table~\ref{tab:topic-k50}) uses a single value of $\lambda_{\text{relevance}}$, held fixed across every dataset and $K$, rather than a value selected per dataset against the $C_V$ being reported. Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-config} reports the earlier per-dataset selection as a separate sensitivity reference, labeled as an upper bound rather than this paper's main result. Document Clustering and Classification involve no such search for any model: VAE-BM reuses its Topic Modeling configuration unchanged, and every baseline is run once with its own official implementation's default settings.

\subsection{Topic modeling protocol}
$K\in\{50,100\}$~\citep{wu2023ecrtm,hicot2025}; coherence ($C_V$, Palmetto against a Wikipedia reference corpus) and topic diversity (TD) alongside label-informed Purity and NMI.

\subsection{Document clustering protocol}
Transductive: each model fits and is evaluated on the full corpus, $K$ set to the dataset's own class count. VAE-BM instantiates the clustering step of \S\ref{sec:dec} with $k$-means. We report every clustering metric we compute: label-based ACC (Hungarian-matched), NMI, ARI, AMI, Homogeneity, Completeness, V-measure, and Purity, plus label-free Silhouette, Davies-Bouldin, and Calinski-Harabasz. The label-based metrics are this paper's primary cross-model evidence; Silhouette, Davies-Bouldin, and Calinski-Harabasz are reported in full (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-cluster-full}) but not used to rank models against each other, since each model's representation has its own geometry and scale (raw embedding space, topic-simplex, TF-IDF, etc.), and an internal metric computed in one such space is not comparable to the same metric computed in another.

\subsection{Classification protocol}
A stratified random 80/20 split (drawn once per seed) is computed first; every model's representation -- vocabulary, topic-model network, encoder, or clustering step, as applicable -- is then fit on the training documents \emph{only}, with test-document representations obtained by a separate inference step that never re-fits on test data. This holds for all eight models, including VAE-BM. An SVM is trained only on training-split labels and representations, and scored on the held-out 20\%. VAE-BM uses 5 seeds (1--5), since which documents land in train vs.\ test genuinely varies by seed; every baseline uses a single seed (42), matching this task's own established single-seed baseline convention -- we report this asymmetry explicitly (\S\ref{sec:limitations}) rather than fabricating repeated-seed uncertainty for a baseline we only ran once.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Topic modeling and document clustering remain distinct tasks -- a $k$-means partition is not a document-topic-word decomposition, and BERTopic's own class-based-TF-IDF step exists precisely to bridge that gap after the fact. But \citet{zhang2022neuraltopicclustering} and \citet{sia2020tired} are right that the practical distance between the two traditions has shrunk, and our own results echo it in a specific way: VAE-BM beats every topic-model-family baseline we compare against, on both experiments, almost everywhere, while its one genuinely mixed comparison (against BERTopic, the one baseline that itself leans on embedding clustering) is a much closer contest. This is consistent with the finding in \S\ref{sec:topic-results}: VAE-BM's representation, at its locked configuration, is architecturally close to a frozen pretrained embedding, so it should be expected to compete on embedding-clustering's own terms specifically. What a pure embedding-clustering pipeline lacks and VAE-BM still provides is a genuine document-topic-word decomposition (\S\ref{sec:topics}) -- though, as \S\ref{sec:topics} states explicitly, the specific topic-word criterion that produces this paper's own results reads cluster word counts, not the trained decoder's parameters; the decoder's own energy view is trained and available but not what this paper's results credit.

\paragraph{Why not the decoder's own energy view?} \S\ref{sec:topics} defines an energy-based scoring rule directly from the trained decoder's $\mathbf{R},\mathbf{b}$, as an alternative to the frequency-based criterion this paper actually reports results with. We compared the two on the metric that matters for topic quality, $C_V$, and found the energy view no better on the datasets tested, so we did not adopt it; the reconstruction objective still shapes $\mathbf{R},\mathbf{b}$ during training regardless of $\alpha$, and this energy view remains available in our implementation, but it is not what this paper's own topic-word results credit. Whether it earns its keep at other points in the architecture's design space ($\alpha>0$, or the appendix ablations below) is a question this paper's own evidence does not settle.

The appendix ablations (Product-of-Experts fusion, jointly-trained clustering) were both attempts to recover some of that architectural flexibility without sacrificing this connection to the vocabulary. Neither displaced the locked configuration once evaluated on equal footing, which we read as a useful negative result: on the datasets and embedder tested, the benefit of a frozen, high-quality contextual encoder outweighs the benefit of any lexical-branch contribution we tried to combine it with, whether combined by a fixed scalar, an uncertainty-weighted mechanism, or a jointly-trained clustering objective.

\section{Conclusion}

We presented VAE-BM, a variational autoencoder with an energy-based Boltzmann decoder whose two encoder branches -- lexical and contextual -- combine via a scalar mixing weight. A systematic sweep of this weight found that any lexical contribution degrades clustering quality relative to a pure-embedding representation, monotonically and without reversal; we lock the resulting embedding-only configuration throughout this paper. With the document representation fixed, applying LDAvis's relevance score systematically as a topic-word selection criterion improves coherence on every one of HiCOT's five official benchmark datasets and matches or beats HiCOT's own published coherence, purity, and NMI simultaneously on three of five -- with strictly zero effect on clustering, evidence that representation quality, clustering quality, and topic-word quality are separable axes a paper can improve independently. Evaluated on a 12-dataset suite against seven baselines, with every baseline reporting the same full metric battery, VAE-BM wins almost every comparison against topic-model-family baselines and remains competitive against the one embedding-hybrid baseline (BERTopic) -- consistent with the broader finding in recent topic-modeling-versus-clustering literature that strong pretrained-encoder representations are hard to beat, while a probabilistic, interpretable, vocabulary-connected representation like VAE-BM's remains a genuinely competitive alternative.

\section*{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{No single controlled run spans every model.} Results are stitched from separate sweeps, sometimes across two different compute environments (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-compute}), rather than one run containing every model; neither is a repeated-seed estimate of the other, and we report this explicitly rather than implying a single controlled comparison.
\item \textbf{HiCOT and FASTopic both collapse on GoogleNews-family datasets}, in both the cluster and classification experiments, on two independent compute environments -- consistent with a shared degenerate-assignment failure mode we have not root-caused.
\item \textbf{Clustering uses a single seed for every model} (a consistent single-run comparison, not a determinism claim -- several neural baselines have their own stochastic training); \textbf{classification uses 5 seeds for VAE-BM but a single seed for every baseline}, since train/test splits genuinely vary by seed. We draw no statistical-significance conclusions from either single-run comparison, and report both asymmetries explicitly rather than fabricating repeated-seed uncertainty for baselines we ran once.
\item \textbf{Appendix ablations use a smaller, older configuration.} The Product-of-Experts and jointly-trained-clustering ablations use a different embedder on HiCOT's five benchmark datasets only, not this paper's locked configuration or 12-dataset suite -- exploratory evidence about the fusion and clustering mechanisms, not a like-for-like comparison.
\item \textbf{RQ2's VAE-BM configuration is an unresolved, disclosed deviation} (\S\ref{sec:rq2}): the configuration used everywhere else in this paper collapses under FASTopic's protocol specifically, and we do not yet know why. This is an open question, not a considered design choice.
\item \textbf{RQ3's HiCOT/Tweet result is a genuine, unresolved negative result} (\S\ref{sec:rq3}): even after both a training-budget fix and a dedicated \texttt{weight\_loss\_DT} sensitivity check, HiCOT recovers only 12 of 89 requested clusters on this dataset. We report this as-is rather than search further against our own unsupervised-selection discipline.
\item \textbf{RQ4's Experiment B is a partial result at paper-freeze time} (\S\ref{sec:rq4}): Experiment A is complete for all 9 backbone/dataset pairs; Experiment B is complete for 6 of 9 (SearchSnippets not yet run). We report exactly which cells are done or missing (Table~\ref{tab:rq4_partial}) rather than extrapolate from the completed cells to the missing ones.
\item \textbf{RQ1's multi-seed rerun found near-zero seed-to-seed variance} (\S\ref{sec:topic-results}): the reported means are not an average over meaningfully different fits for four of five datasets (IMDB alone showed a small, non-zero std). We disclose this rather than imply genuine stochastic averaging occurred.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Ethics Statement}
\label{sec:ethics}
All datasets used in this paper are public, previously-published benchmarks (see Table~\ref{tab:datasets} and \S\ref{sec:rq3}), used here only for topic modeling, clustering, and classification evaluation, consistent with their original release terms. RQ4's LLM-assisted refinement uses a small, publicly released open-weight model (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) run locally under our own control, with no data sent to a third-party API; the refinement step operates only on topic words and cluster assignments already produced by the base models, and introduces no new personal or sensitive information into any dataset. We are not aware of a specific harm pathway distinct from the general, well-documented risks of topic modeling and text clustering over public corpora (e.g., downstream misuse for surveillance or profiling), which this paper does not newly enable.

\section{Exploratory Fusion and Clustering Ablations}
\label{sec:appendix-ablation}
This appendix reports two exploratory variants that do \emph{not} appear as our proposed model anywhere in the main text: an uncertainty-weighted encoder fusion, and a jointly-trained clustering objective. Both were evaluated on HiCOT's five benchmark datasets under an older configuration (\texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2}, not the locked \texttt{gte-large}/\texttt{bge-large} setup used for every main-text VAE-BM result) and neither displaced this paper's locked fixed fusion (Equation~\ref{eq:final-fusion}) as its proposed configuration.

\subsection{Product-of-Experts fusion}
Instead of the fixed scalar $\alpha$ of Equation~\ref{eq:alpha-fusion}, a Product-of-Experts (PoE) combination~\citep{hinton2002poe} fuses the two branch posteriors, together with the standard-normal prior, by precision:
\begin{equation}
q_{\mathrm{PoE}}(\mathbf{z}\mid\mathbf{x}_b,\mathbf{x}_e) \;\propto\; p(\mathbf{z})\,q_b(\mathbf{z}\mid\mathbf{x}_b)\,q_e(\mathbf{z}\mid\mathbf{x}_e).
\label{eq:poe-def}
\end{equation}
For diagonal Gaussians this product is itself Gaussian and closed-form. Writing each branch's precision as $\boldsymbol{\tau}_b=\boldsymbol{\sigma}_b^{-2}$ and $\boldsymbol{\tau}_e=\boldsymbol{\sigma}_e^{-2}$, the fused mean is the precision-weighted average of the two branch means and the prior's zero mean:
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathrm{PoE}} = \frac{\boldsymbol{\tau}_b\,\boldsymbol{\mu}_b+\boldsymbol{\tau}_e\,\boldsymbol{\mu}_e}{\mathbf{1}+\boldsymbol{\tau}_b+\boldsymbol{\tau}_e}.
\label{eq:poe-mean}
\end{equation}
Whichever branch reports lower variance (higher confidence) for a given document and latent dimension dominates Equation~\ref{eq:poe-mean} for exactly that document and dimension, automatically -- in contrast to Equation~\ref{eq:alpha-fusion}'s $\alpha$, which is identical for every document in the corpus. We refer to this variant as VAE-BM-PoE.

\subsection{Jointly-trained clustering}
As an alternative to the two-stage $k$-means of \S\ref{sec:dec}, VAE-BM-DEC trains cluster centroids $\mathbf{c}_1,\dots,\mathbf{c}_K$ jointly with the encoder/decoder, following Deep Embedded Clustering~\citep{xie2016dec}. A Student-$t$ kernel gives each point's soft assignment to centroid $k$:
\begin{equation}
q_{ik} = \frac{\left(1+\|\mathbf{z}_i-\mathbf{c}_k\|^2\right)^{-1}}{\sum_j\left(1+\|\mathbf{z}_i-\mathbf{c}_j\|^2\right)^{-1}}.
\label{eq:dec-q}
\end{equation}
This is sharpened once per epoch into a target distribution that up-weights high-confidence assignments,
\begin{equation}
p_{ik} = \frac{q_{ik}^2/f_k}{\sum_j q_{ij}^2/f_j}, \qquad f_k=\sum_i q_{ik},
\label{eq:dec-p}
\end{equation}
and an additional loss term, weighted by $\lambda_c=0.1$, penalizes the KL divergence between target and soft assignment:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{KL}(P\|Q) = \sum_i\sum_k p_{ik}\log\frac{p_{ik}}{q_{ik}}.
\label{eq:dec-kl}
\end{equation}
Centroids are initialized via $k$-means on the untrained encoder's own $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ before the first optimization step. Final cluster assignment is $y_d=\arg\max_k q_{dk}$, not a separate $k$-means call.

\subsection{Ablation result}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} compares fixed-$\alpha$ fusion ($\alpha=0.99$, VAE-BM's own original default -- this ablation isolates the fusion \emph{mechanism} at the weight VAE-BM was originally proposed with, separately from the $\alpha$-value finding of \S\ref{sec:encoder}), VAE-BM-PoE, and VAE-BM-DEC on HiCOT's five benchmark datasets. VAE-BM-PoE and VAE-BM-DEC's cluster-experiment checkpoints were selected using the same ground-truth labels the experiment ultimately reports accuracy against (an oracle, label-informed selection, since the cluster experiment is transductive and has no held-out split to select on instead); the fixed-$\alpha$ row uses no such selection.
